Clos-ULCF Results

Replacing the 96 × 1 aggregation coupler in each WR switch with K couplers, K EDFAs and a pair of ULCF AWGs cuts the loss before the EDFA by up to 14 dB. The required transmitter power drops by up to 13 dB at every size from 1536 to 12288 ports.

The largest switch, 12288 × 12288 (2457.6 Tbps), works at −15 dBm with Scheme A1, and at −12 dBm with the cheaper Scheme A2 under dispersed traffic, where the conventional A2 switch is infeasible.

N = 96 wavelengths, 50-GHz grid M = 16–128 ports = 96 × M 32-GBd DP-16QAM, 200 Gb/s BER ≤ 1e-2 feasible: P_TX ≤ 5 dBm

  • Selectors per port are unchanged: 4(M − 1) for A1, 3(M − 1) for A2.
  • Each WR switch needs K EDFAs, K splitters and 2 AWGs instead of 1 EDFA and 1 AWG.
  • Because A2 becomes feasible at 12288 ports, the proposal can save 127 selectors per port against A1.

Main limitations

  • Traffic dependence. With A2 and concentrated traffic every third-stage leak is in band again; the gain shrinks with M and 12288 ports stay infeasible.
  • New crosstalk floor. K − 1 ULCF AWG leaks at −32 dB cap the SNR near 21.4 dB at K = 12. Larger K adds little; K = 8 already gives most of the gain.
  • Simulation only. No device was fabricated or measured. The 32-dB AWG crosstalk comes from the lab code.

Significance

  • The aggregation coupler loss, the main scalability limit of the JLT architecture, is reduced without changing the space switches.
  • The saving buys either far lower transmitter power or the simpler Scheme A2 at the largest size.
  • A connection's wavelength depends only on its source group and destination, so rearrangement needs no laser retuning.
  • Third-stage in-band leaks fall from M − 1 to ⌈M/K⌉ − 1 under dispersed traffic.
