1. With selectors in the third stage (Scheme A1) the proposed switch lowers the required transmitter power by 3, 7, 10 and 13 dB for K = 2, 4, 8 and 12 at every size, for dispersed and for concentrated traffic alike: the third-stage leaks pass two selectors (SXR above 49 dB) and never matter, so this whole gain is the middle stage (coupler saving 3.5 to 14 dB, minus 0.5 to 1 dB for the ULCF pair: splitter, passband deviation, AWG crosstalk).
2. With couplers in the third stage (Scheme A2) and dispersed traffic the gain splits into the two sources the task asks for: at the required power of the conventional switch and K = 12 the middle stage adds 4.1 / 2.7 / 0.7 / 0.9 dB of SNR and the leaks on other wavelengths add 1.7 / 2.8 / 3.8 / 4.4 dB for M = 16 / 32 / 64 / 128; the second source grows with M because the stage-3 crosstalk of the conventional A2 switch grows with M.
3. The practical consequence is that Scheme A2 (25 percent fewer selector elements) becomes feasible at 12288 ports: -8 dBm with K = 8 and -12 dBm with K = 12, where the conventional A2 switch is infeasible even at 10 dBm; the proposal can buy the simpler third stage instead of more power.
4. With concentrated traffic the third-stage leaks are all in band again and the gain of Scheme A2 shrinks with M and turns into a loss: at 6144 ports the required power goes from 4 dBm (conventional) to above 10 dBm (K = 2), -1 (K = 4), -7 (K = 8) and -9 dBm (K = 12), and 12288 ports stay infeasible for every K; the reason is that the EDFA output per channel is fixed by its saturation power, so the extra 6.5 dB of the ULCF pair and splitter in front of the 24 to 28 dB third-stage coupler and the pre-amplifier caps the SNR (13.45 dB at K = 2) regardless of transmitter power.
5. The ULCF AWG crosstalk (K-1 leaks at 32 dB, SXR 21.4 dB at K = 12) is the term that limits the proposed switch with dispersed traffic in every case but A2 at 12288 ports, where the pre-amplifier noise limits; a better AWG crosstalk, not more input ports, is the next lever, and K = 8 already gives most of the gain.
6. The price is hardware in the middle stage: K EDFAs, two AWGs and K splitters per WR switch instead of one EDFA and one AWG (0.125 instead of 0.0104 EDFAs per port at K = 12), while the selector count per port is unchanged; against 252 to 508 selector elements per port this is a small addition, and with A2 it saves 63 to 127 selector elements per port.
7. The two-stage switch on the same parameter set reaches 768 ports at 3 dBm with K = 1 (the paper: 384 ports at 0 dBm) and 3072 ports at 0 dBm with K = 12, but 6144 ports need 6 dBm and 12288 are out of reach, so it does not compete with the three-stage switches above about 3000 ports.
8. The advantage of the proposal is gone when the traffic is concentrated and the third stage uses couplers; with selectors in the third stage the advantage does not depend on the traffic at all. It would also be gone if the wavelength of a connection depended on the middle-stage switch (groups not identical in every WR switch), because then every rearrangement would need a laser retune.
9. All required powers carry a resolution of 1 dB and a random error of one grid step at points near the FEC limit (seed 2 against seed 1: 63 of 71 entries equal, the rest differ by 1 dB); differences of 1 dB between curves are not significant, differences of 3 dB and more are.
