=== Answers to the questions of the task ===
- What changes the wavelength: the first-stage switch (through its group) and the
  third-stage switch. The WR switch does not.
- Division into groups: any division into K groups of N/K switches works. The rule
  and the leak counts only use the group index, so the division does not matter for
  the counts; it decides which racks share a wavelength at a given destination.
- Likely traffic in a datacenter: dispersed (a rack talks to many racks). Worst case:
  concentrated, which gives the conventional count M - 1 again.
- A leak on another wavelength is (g2 - g1) channels away: at least 1 channel = 50 GHz.
  At 32 GBd the receive band ends at 16 GHz, so the nearest leak is 34 GHz outside it.
- First-stage leaks: the M inputs of first-stage switch i are all in group g(i); their
  wavelengths differ when their destinations differ, exactly as in the conventional
  switch. step22 takes the worst case (all in band) and so does work2: no change.
- Receiver: output j can receive K different wavelengths, one per group. A NEW
  connection from another group needs the local oscillator of the receiver retuned
  (frequency selection, > 100 us). A REARRANGEMENT never does: only selectors switch.
